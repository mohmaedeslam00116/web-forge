# Design Direction — choose a visual world, not a theme preset

The selected direction is the visible consequence of user tasks, content, product world, culture, brand truth, and the intended emotional response. Do not equate quality with a quiet neutral layout. Use `visual-art-direction` as the primary decision method.

## Required decisions

1. Surface mode and ambition: Persuade, Operate, Read, or Experience; restrained, composed, expressive, or immersive.
2. Visual family: choose from the catalog or create a justified hybrid; never start from a default industry palette.
3. Source world: concrete cultural/material systems that resonate with the product; avoid near-duplicates and obvious category defaults.
4. Color: restrained, committed, full palette, drenched, or image-led. State which large regions carry the color and what it communicates.
5. Image/media language: photography, product capture, illustration, 3D, maps, charts, diagrams, video, texture, or a justified text-only approach.
6. Type: choose typefaces for voice, content, language support, and licensing; typography supports the visual world but must not be the only instrument by habit.
7. Geometry/material/depth: describe borders, surfaces, lighting, layering, grain, paper, glass, metal, fabric, or other materials only when relevant.
8. Motion: describe what motion clarifies, the level of ambition, reduced-motion fallback, and performance cost.
9. Signature visual: one recognizable visual device that helps the user understand or remember the product.

## Reference rules

Use `brand-reference-engineering` for first-party and community DESIGN.md references. A reference can inform composition, image treatment, color behavior, typography, and interaction, but do not copy proprietary assets or treat community analysis as official. Record sources and rejected patterns in the Design Passport.

## Anti-minimalism check

Before finalizing, ask:
- Is this genuinely right for the user's task, or did I choose restrained because it is easy?
- Does the first viewport show the product/artifact or a compelling visual explanation when the surface needs one?
- Would photography, illustration, a diagram, a product capture, a material field, or a richer palette improve comprehension or emotion?
- Are the three concepts materially different in visual family, ambition, and media—not just typography and color?

If the result is all typography, whitespace, thin rules, and muted surfaces, it needs a specific product reason and must survive comparison with more visually expressive candidates.

## Output

A concise direction contract: mode/ambition, visual family and source world, palette strategy, media language, typography roles, materials/depth, graphic rules, motion, signature visual, deliberate exclusions, and reference provenance.
