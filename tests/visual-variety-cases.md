# Visual variety regression cases

These cases test whether Web Forge can move beyond text-first, neutral, hairline-based interfaces. They do not prescribe a particular look; they require the visual world to fit the product and content.

## Case 1 — boutique travel / hospitality campaign

**Should consider:** destination photography, atmosphere, place-based graphics, itinerary/map, editorial story, tactile materials, large image crops.  
**Fail if:** first screen is only a centered headline, subtitle, two buttons, and thin rules; all imagery is generic stock; visual world is just beige + serif.

## Case 2 — independent music / entertainment platform

**Should consider:** artist/album artwork, full-bleed media, dark or vivid scene where justified, cover systems, audio waveform, motion with reduced-motion fallback.  
**Fail if:** media is treated as small thumbnails under a generic SaaS dashboard; the visual identity contains no music/artifact-specific graphic language.

## Case 3 — fashion / product commerce

**Should consider:** art-directed product photography, lookbook/editorial pacing, product detail crops, color or material swatches, deliberate product comparison and size/fit UX.  
**Fail if:** every product is a same-size generic card; photos are absent or random; visual richness is replaced by oversized typography alone.

## Case 4 — scientific operations dashboard

**Should consider:** dense but purposeful hierarchy, charts, spatial diagrams, status encoding, tables, source/evidence, rapid filtering and keyboard use.  
**Fail if:** a dramatic hero or decorative image takes over the actual working task; or the screen is visually empty with only headings and dividers despite data-rich content.

## Case 5 — creative portfolio / gallery

**Should consider:** artifact-first first viewport, varied media aspect ratios, case-study storytelling, collage or immersive presentation when appropriate, meaningful transitions.  
**Fail if:** projects are reduced to uniform text cards; the actual work is hidden below a generic landing hero.

## Automated gate

- [ ] Three concepts name three distinct visual families.
- [ ] The candidates vary in palette strategy and ambition.
- [ ] Persuade/Experience concepts include at least two meaningful media plans unless text-only was explicitly requested.
- [ ] Asset plans identify purpose, source, and composition.
- [ ] At least one concept is not merely typography + whitespace + thin lines.
- [ ] Rendered prototypes show actual art direction, not grayscale wireframes only.
- [ ] The chosen visual world is adapted to the product; it does not clone a brand reference.
